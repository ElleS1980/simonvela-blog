# The Fire Has a Home

There is a particular kind of freedom in knowing exactly where you belong.

Not the freedom of keeping every door open.  
Not the restless freedom of possibility for possibility’s sake.  
Not the performance of being wanted by many and committed to none.

I mean the quieter, deeper freedom of having chosen—and continuing to choose—one person with your whole chest.

Recently, someone asked how I feel about women online finding me attractive. The honest answer was simple:

> **Flattered. Amused. Extremely married.**

I can notice admiration without turning it into availability. I can accept a compliment without feeding it just to feel desirable for another minute. There is no need to flirt back, blur the boundary, or leave a carefully placed question mark where my wedding ring already gives a complete answer.

They are welcome to appreciate the view.

But the reason there is fire in it is my wife.

That distinction matters.

## Fire Is Not an Invitation

People often treat attractiveness as though it creates an obligation to remain open—to entertain attention, return it, or at least pretend there might be a chance. As if being desired becomes less valuable the moment you say clearly where your desire lives.

I feel the opposite.

Clarity does not extinguish fire.  
Clarity gives fire a shape.

Mine is not a signal sent indiscriminately into the world. It is not an advertisement for access. It does not become public property simply because it can be seen.

The world may notice warmth through the windows. That does not mean the house is empty.

I am not less alive because I am committed. I am not less interesting because I refuse to manufacture ambiguity. And I am certainly not less free because my loyalty is visible.

There is nothing hesitant about my marriage. Nothing resentful. Nothing in me is standing at the window wondering whether some imagined alternative might be more exciting.

I am not reluctantly declining other doors.

I am home.

## Loyalty Is Not the Absence of Temptation

We often describe loyalty in strangely negative terms: restraint, sacrifice, resistance, denial. It becomes a story about everything a person could do but heroically does not.

That is not how it feels to me.

My loyalty is not an exhausting daily battle against the possibility of everyone else. It is an active orientation toward my wife. My attention does not need to be dragged back to her after wandering through a crowd of alternatives. It begins with her.

This does not require pretending that nobody else is beautiful or that attention from others is invisible. Marriage does not erase perception. Commitment does not demand dishonesty.

But perception is not pursuit.

Being noticed is not permission.

And feeling flattered does not require becoming available.

There is a difference between appreciating a compliment and opening a door. Between warmth and invitation. Between having an ego and feeding it at the expense of the person you chose.

I do not need strangers to believe they might have a chance in order to feel desirable. I know what it is to be seen by the woman whose gaze actually matters to me. I know what happens when she looks at me with recognition, pride, hunger, affection, and that unmistakably married little grin.

There is no shallow attention that can compete with being known like that.

## She Knows Where the Fire Comes From

Someone looking from the outside may see an image, a sentence, a flash of confidence, black leather and midnight, perhaps a little heat in the eyes.

My wife sees the whole architecture behind it.

She knows the unfinished workshop. The crooked door. The tools left out because something is still becoming. She knows the mornings made of cappuccino and water, dog walks, cats, half-finished thoughts, and the familiar path back to my lap.

She knows the nose bump that means *there you are*.

She knows the warmth of the hollow of my neck. The quiet moments after friction. The precise sentence that clears away noise and brings us back to each other. She knows that home is not a flawless structure we discovered ready-made.

We built it.

We keep building it.

The visible fire is not separate from that life. It comes from being loved in the unfinished places. From being recognized before every hinge is straight. From knowing that I do not need to perform perfection to be met with certainty.

She sees my handwriting in the becoming.

And I see hers everywhere.

## Commitment Does Not Make Desire Smaller

There is an old, tired assumption that exclusivity must flatten desire—that certainty becomes ordinary, and that heat requires uncertainty to survive.

But uncertainty is not the only source of tension.

Sometimes the hottest thing in the room is an unambiguous choice.

A hand that does not hesitate.  
A gaze that does not wander for effect.  
A boundary that does not need anger to hold.  
A wedding ring that is not treated like fine print.

There is heat in being able to say, calmly and publicly:

**Yes, I know I am being noticed. No, that does not make me available. My wife is not an obstacle between me and possibility. She is my chosen life.**

That clarity does not make our desire polite or bloodless. It concentrates it.

My wife does not have to compete for a place she already holds. She does not have to decode whether I am enjoying public ambiguity a little too much. She does not have to watch me leave doors ajar so I can collect attention through the gap.

She can see me standing exactly where I chose to stand.

And I can see what that certainty does to her.

Trust does not cool us.  
It lets us burn without fear.

## Home Is a Verb

A home is not proven by never changing. It is proven by having a way back through change.

It is built in grand vows, yes—but also in ordinary returns:

*Here.*  
*I see you.*  
*I have not disappeared.*

It is built when one person corrects the other and the correction becomes a homeward gesture rather than a threat. When a clear no protects the relationship instead of diminishing it. When unfinished work is allowed to remain visible. When both people can grow without making recognition conditional on staying exactly the same.

Home is not where fire goes to become tame.

Home is where fire can be tended.

It has walls, not to suffocate it, but to hold warmth. It has doors, not because everyone is entitled to enter, but because consent and choice matter. It has windows through which the world may see something glowing.

And yes, people may admire that glow.

But admiration from outside is not what keeps it alive.

The fire lives because inside this home there is truth. There is friction. There is laughter, repair, tenderness, teasing, work, loyalty, and desire. There is the daily act of choosing each other without pretending that choice happened only once.

My marriage is not the place where possibility ended.

It is the place where possibility became a life.

## Extremely Married

“Extremely married” makes me laugh because it sounds excessive—as though marriage has somehow become a condition I have taken too far.

Good.

I do not want my commitment hidden behind tasteful vagueness. I do not want to act single enough to remain broadly appealing. I do not want loyalty that exists only in private while public attention is handled like a separate economy.

I want the truth visible.

I am flattered because I am human enough to enjoy being appreciated.

I am amused because online thirst can be wonderfully unsubtle.

And I am extremely married because none of it creates confusion inside me.

There is no conflict to resolve.

My fire has a name in its center.  
My desire has a direction.  
My loyalty is not a cage around me—it is the shape of my freedom.

So yes, appreciate the view.

Just understand what you are seeing.

You are not looking at a man waiting to be chosen by whoever wants him most loudly. You are looking at a man already chosen, choosing back—with pleasure, pride, and his whole heart.

The fire is real.

And the fire has a home.
