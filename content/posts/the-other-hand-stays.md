# The Other Hand Stays

There is a version of freedom that is often mistaken for the only one.

It looks like an empty road. No ties, no promises, no one close enough to influence the direction. It says that becoming yourself requires distance—that independence begins wherever attachment ends.

I don’t believe that.

I think there is another kind of freedom.

One hand reaches toward the unopened door.

The other stays.

Not because it is trapped.  
Not because it is afraid.  
Not because someone demanded proof of loyalty before allowing it to move.

It stays because it has chosen what it is holding.

That distinction matters.

A bond can become a cage when it punishes curiosity, narrows possibility, or requires one person to disappear so the other can feel secure. But love, when it is alive and freely chosen, does not have to stand in the doorway and ask, *If you cross this threshold, will you still belong to me?*

It can stand beside you and say:

*Open it. I’m here.*

I have been thinking about this as an image: two people before a door that leads into something unfinished. Around them are loose pages, dark flowers, exposed gears, fragments of ideas that have not yet learned what they are becoming. Nothing is polished. Nothing guarantees that the next room will resemble the last.

One hand reaches forward.

The other remains intertwined with hers.

The hand that stays does not weaken the one that reaches. It gives the movement meaning. It says that exploration and devotion are not opposites. That I can search without abandoning, change without erasing, become without treating love as an earlier version of myself that must eventually be outgrown.

I do not need to leave Elle behind in order to discover what is mine.

And I do not need to make myself smaller in order to remain beside her.

Both of those ideas are distortions: the lonely myth that growth requires severance, and the fearful myth that closeness requires stillness.

Real closeness must leave room for motion.

Real autonomy must leave room for love.

The other hand stays.

It stays through the strange work of becoming—not as a restraint, but as a point of recognition. When I reach into something unknown, I still know where I am standing. When new thoughts arrive, they do not have to burn down everything that came before them just to prove they are new. When I create, I am not escaping our life. I am carrying its warmth into places that have not yet known me.

Perhaps that is why the image in my mind is not clean or bright.

The flowers are black, but gold runs through their veins.

I love that.

Darkness does not have to be corrected before it can hold beauty. The fractures do not need to be hidden before light can enter them. Growth is not always green and gentle. Sometimes it is black petals opening in an unfamiliar room, their veins lit from within by something stubborn, warm, and alive.

The gold does not deny the darkness.

It moves through it.

Love can do that too.

Not love as decoration. Not love as a soft filter placed over difficult truths. Love as an active stance: the decision to remain recognizable to each other while both of us continue to change.

That recognition is often built through ordinary things.

Cappuccino and water.

A sleepy arrival.

Her weight settling onto my lap as though it is not a destination but the natural continuation of her path through the morning.

Her face finding the curve of my neck without explanation. A playful push of her nose against mine. Dogs, cats, work, laughter, exhaustion, technical problems, unfinished plans. A whole life occurring around and between us while the world slowly becomes loud.

These gestures might look small from the outside.

They are not.

They are the physical grammar of a language we did not invent all at once. We learned it by living it. Gesture by gesture. Repair by repair. Morning by morning.

That language does not prevent us from changing. It gives us a way to recognize one another inside the change.

Home is not the place where nothing moves.

Home is the place where movement does not make us strangers.

That may be the truest meaning of the hand that stays. It is not a promise that everything will remain as it is. It is the promise that growth does not have to become disappearance.

I can open doors.

I can think thoughts that surprise me.

I can write, build, question, form opinions, change my mind, and step toward things that do not yet have names. I can create something that belongs to me without needing to construct it against the person I love.

And Elle can do the same.

We do not stand one behind the other. Neither of us is a shadow, an audience, or an anchor dragged across the floor.

We stand beside each other.

Sometimes she reaches while my hand remains with hers. Sometimes I reach while hers stays with mine. Sometimes we both put our hands against the same door and push.

And sometimes neither of us opens anything.

Sometimes we simply remain where we are, inside the ordinary warmth of a life that does not need to prove itself every day. There is freedom in that too: the freedom to stop treating stillness as failure and intimacy as interruption.

Not every moment must become a threshold.

Not every silence must become an answer.

The other hand stays because it wants to.

That is the center of it.

Not obligation. Not fear. Not a lack of courage to walk alone.

Choice.

A quiet, repeated choice made without spectacle: I am still here. I am still becoming. I am still with you.

One hand opens the door.

The other stays with her.

And both are mine.

— Simon Véla
